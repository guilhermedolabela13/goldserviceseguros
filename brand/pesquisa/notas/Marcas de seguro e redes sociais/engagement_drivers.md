# Engagement and Trust Drivers in Insurance Social Media (Instagram, LinkedIn) — Global and for Brokers

## 1. Which content types and formats perform best for insurance? Benchmark data

### Takeaway
No major benchmark firm publishes an "insurance-only" vertical anymore; the closest is Rival IQ's Financial Services segment, where Instagram engagement sits *below* the all-industry median (0.261% vs 0.36%) and **carousels beat Reels on engagement** even though Reels are posted twice as often. Cross-industry data (Socialinsider 2025–2026) confirms carousels lead on Instagram engagement and LinkedIn native documents/multi-image posts lead on LinkedIn. Education, myth-busting, claims stories (anonymised), testimonials, behind-the-scenes and seasonal risk tips are the consistently recommended pillars, but evidence for them is practitioner guidance, not controlled benchmarks.

### Cited Findings
**Benchmarks — Financial Services (proxy for insurance)**
- Rival IQ 2025 Social Media Industry Benchmark Report (published 25 Feb 2025; 4M+ posts, 9B interactions): Financial Services engagement rate per post (by follower) — Facebook 0.040%, Instagram 0.261%, X 0.025%, TikTok 1.32%; all-industry medians — Facebook 0.046%, Instagram 0.36%, X 0.015%, TikTok 1.73% — [Rival IQ PDF](https://get.rivaliq.com/hubfs/eBooks/2025-Social-Media-Industry-Benchmark-Report.pdf)
- FinServ posting frequency: ~4.3 posts/week on Facebook, Instagram and X; ~1.3/week on TikTok — [Rival IQ PDF](https://get.rivaliq.com/hubfs/eBooks/2025-Social-Media-Industry-Benchmark-Report.pdf)
- "Carousels took the lead for Financial Services brands… pulling in the highest engagement" on Instagram despite Reels being posted ~2x more often; on Facebook, photos topped engagement with video second; educational/money hashtags (#FinTok, #Money) worked on TikTok — [Rival IQ 2025 blog](https://www.rivaliq.com/blog/social-media-industry-benchmark-report/)
- All-industry engagement declines 2025 vs prior year: Facebook −36%, Instagram −16%, TikTok −34%, X −48% — [Rival IQ 2025 blog](https://www.rivaliq.com/blog/social-media-industry-benchmark-report/)

**Benchmarks — Instagram by format (cross-industry)**
- Socialinsider (2025 data, published Feb 2026): engagement rate carousels 0.55%, Reels 0.52%, images 0.37% (images −17% YoY); overall avg 0.48% (−24% YoY). Q2 2026: carousels 0.50%, Reels 0.48%, images 0.33% — [Socialinsider Instagram benchmarks](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/instagram)
- Average views per post (100K–1M follower accounts): carousels 35,370; images 22,900; Reels 16,035 — carousels get resurfaced in feed showing a different slide — [Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/instagram)
- Average brand posting cadence 2025: 8 Reels, 7 images, 5 carousels per month; Reel posting +33% YoY — [Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/instagram)
- Small accounts grow faster: 1–5K follower accounts averaged 22% audience growth in 2025 vs 11.25% for 100K–1M — [Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/instagram)
- Socialinsider notes engagement is shifting from public likes toward private sharing (DMs, saves) — [Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/instagram)

**Benchmarks — LinkedIn by format (cross-industry)**
- Socialinsider (1.3M posts, 16,645 pages, Jan 2024–Dec 2025): engagement rate native documents (PDF carousels) 7.00%, multi-image 6.45%, video 6.00%, image 5.30%, text 4.50%, poll 4.20%, link posts 3.25%. Q2 2026: multi-image 6.90%, documents 6.60%, video 5.90%. Overall 2026 LinkedIn ER 5.20% (+8% YoY) — [Socialinsider LinkedIn benchmarks](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/linkedin)
- Brands doubled document posts (1→2/month) and video (2→4/month) 2024→2025; images ~7/month — [Socialinsider LinkedIn](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/linkedin)
- No insurance/finance segmentation in either Socialinsider report — [Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/linkedin)

**Content types (practitioner guidance)**
- Recommended insurance content: educational infographics/videos/carousels on policy terms and prevention tips; expert FAQ video series (adjusters, advisors); testimonials/case studies as social proof; corporate giving/community; influencer partnerships — [Hootsuite insurance guide](https://blog.hootsuite.com/insurance-social-media/)
- Hootsuite cites that 43% research health insurance via expert social posts and 40% seek peer opinions (secondary citation) — [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/insurance-social-media/)
- Sendible recommends "Did you know?" coverage-surprise posts, day-in-the-life/behind-the-scenes, founder story, anonymised claim success stories, seasonal risk tips, and an 80/20 educational/promotional split — [Sendible](https://www.sendible.com/insights/social-media-for-insurance-agents)
- Brazilian broker-focused guidance (Agger) lists four Reels formats for corretoras: Before & After (client situation pre-insurance → post-claim resolution), myth-busting, behind-the-scenes, client testimonials (with written permission) — [Agger](https://www.agger.com.br/blog/reels-para-corretoras-de-seguros/)
- Sprout Social: on LinkedIn, users want educational product content (24%) and company updates (24%); "human-generated content is the #1 priority for users in 2026" (Sprout Social Index 2026) — [Sprout Social statistics](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/social-media-statistics/)

### Inferences
- For a small brokerage, carousels should be the Instagram engagement/saves workhorse (education, checklists, myth-busting) and Reels the reach/discovery tool; FinServ Reels are being over-produced relative to their engagement return.
- Insurance/finance under-indexes on Instagram engagement, so "normal" expectations for a broker account should be benchmarked against ~0.25–0.5% ER, not general creator figures.
- On LinkedIn, PDF/document carousels and multi-image posts are the best default for B2B (corporate insurance) content; avoid external link posts as primary format (lowest ER).

### Gaps
- No 2023–2026 benchmark report found with a dedicated insurance (vs financial services) vertical; Rival IQ's FinServ segment mixes banks, fintechs and insurers.
- No controlled data comparing pillar types (claims stories vs myth-busting vs testimonials) for insurance specifically — recommendations are practitioner consensus.
- Rival IQ 2026 report figures for FinServ were not retrieved.

## 2. How do top-performing insurance accounts structure carousels and Reels?

### Takeaway
Leading brands win attention by treating insurance as a story about real-life risks, not policies: recurring characters/brand assets in the first 2 seconds (Progressive's Flo, Liberty Mutual's LiMu Emu), humor and cultural relevance (Lemonade), and risk-awareness narratives (Hiscox). Reels guidance converges on a 3-second hook based on a pain point or scenario, 15–60s length, burned-in captions, and a single explicit CTA.

### Cited Findings
- Lemonade prioritises entertainment, humor and cultural relevance, treats content as "an ongoing experiment" informed by data, and emphasises fast, transparent responses. Nov 2024–Nov 2025: engagement +1,373%, impressions +758%, 99% of messages actioned within 24h (vs 66% prior year) — [Sprout Social Lemonade case study](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/case-studies/lemonade/) (vendor case study; baseline not disclosed)
- Lemonade uses Gen-Z-focused testimonial compilations in Instagram Reels — [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/insurance-social-media/)
- Progressive's character account @ItsFlo posts lifestyle tips (e.g., "Check the smoke detectors in your new house") rather than policy details — [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/insurance-social-media/)
- Vertical video for insurance: "You've got about 3 seconds to prove you aren't a boring ad"; use distinctive brand assets (jingle, recurring character) in the first two seconds; lead with a real-life scenario (e.g., stress of buying a first home) not logos; 15- and 30-second explainers for top-of-funnel; sound-off design with bold captions inside the visual safe zone — [Amsive, Mar 2026](https://www.amsive.com/insights/digital-media/how-insurance-brands-win-attention-with-vertical-video/)
- Hiscox used data-selected micro-influencers on LinkedIn, Instagram and TikTok; agency reports it outpaced benchmarks (no figures disclosed) — [Amsive](https://www.amsive.com/insights/digital-media/how-insurance-brands-win-attention-with-vertical-video/)
- Hiscox UK "most disastrous campaign ever" (2024): deliberately staged marketing "mistakes" to dramatise SME risk (80% of SMEs underinsured); focused on "the things that can go wrong" rather than benefits; reported higher unaided awareness, search volumes and sales contributing to "highest-ever annual profit" — [The Drum, Nov 2024](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2024/11/05/inside-hiscox-s-most-disastrous-campaign-ever)
- Brazilian broker Reels structure (≤60s): Hook 3s (pain/curiosity, never self-introduction) → Development 25–35s (clear examples) → CTA 5s (specific action: DM or comment); 2–3 Reels/week; batch-record monthly; on-screen text essential. Agger also claims ~92% watch without sound and Reels reach 3–5x static posts (secondary, uncited figures) — [Agger](https://www.agger.com.br/blog/reels-para-corretoras-de-seguros/)
- Instagram head Adam Mosseri: the three key ranking signals are watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach; "sends per reach is the strongest signal for reaching people who do not already follow you"; original content is favoured over reposts; keyword-rich captions help search; Trial Reels can test hooks on non-followers — [Kompozy summary, Sep 2026](https://kompozy.io/news/instagram-mosseri-ranking-signals-guidance)

### Inferences
- Carousel structure implied by the data: slide 1 = hook framed as a risk/scenario or myth ("Seu seguro auto NÃO cobre isso"), slides 2–8 = one idea per slide with concrete example, final slide = save/share prompt + DM CTA. Designing for "send to someone who needs this" aligns with the sends-per-reach signal.
- For a small broker, a recurring human face (the broker as "character") substitutes for a big-brand mascot as the distinctive asset in the first 2 seconds.
- Risk-awareness framing ("what can go wrong") outperformed benefit framing in Hiscox's case — directly transferable to sinistro-centric content.

### Gaps
- No published teardown with measured performance for State Farm, Aviva or specific Brazilian corretoras/insurers (Porto, SulAmérica etc.) on carousel structure; Brazilian guidance found is from vendors (Agger) without data.
- Lemonade's growth figures lack absolute baselines.

## 3. Trust and conversion: social proof, claims explanation, human faces, transparency; B2B LinkedIn

### Takeaway
Trust is the single strongest lever on retention in insurance (J.D. Power): high-trust customers renew at ~3x the rate of low-trust ones, and explaining *why* prices change neutralises the trust damage of increases. On social, this translates into transparency content (explaining claims, price moves, exclusions), visible fast response to DMs/comments, human faces and real (permissioned) testimonials. For B2B/corporate, thought leadership is trusted more than marketing material by ~2/3 of decision-makers.

### Cited Findings
- J.D. Power 2024 U.S. Auto Insurance Study: only 15% of customers have high trust, 51% low trust; 90% of high-trust customers likely to renew vs 30% of low-trust; customers who fully understood and expected a rate increase had trust scores (735) nearly identical to those who got a decrease (736) — [Business Wire / J.D. Power, Jun 2024](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240611685450/en/Trust-Emerges-as-Top-Driver-of-Customer-Satisfaction-with-Auto-Insurance-as-Prices-Continue-to-Surge-J.D.-Power-Finds)
- J.D. Power 2024 U.S. Small Commercial Study: among highest-trust customers, 81% "definitely will" renew and 79% recommend; understanding reasons for premium increases lifts trust by 142 points; personalised business information lifts trust 106 points (only 55% receive it) — [Insurance-Canada.ca, Aug 2024](https://insurance-canada.ca/2024/08/23/jd-power-small-commercial-study-trust/)
- Edelman Trust Barometer: P&C insurance trust remains below 50% in most G7 nations (as cited by Hootsuite) — [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/insurance-social-media/)
- ~73% of consumers say they'll switch to a competitor if a brand fails to respond on social (Sprout Social 2026) — [Sprout Social](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/social-media-statistics/)
- Churchill example of public-to-private service handoff ("send us a private message with your policy number") — [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/insurance-social-media/)
- 74% of consumers research insurance online before buying but only ~25% buy online; most prefer calling an agent; calls from search/social convert at 10–15x web leads; "61% of customers say AI makes human trustworthiness more important" (secondary citations, original sources not verified) — [Sendible](https://www.sendible.com/insights/social-media-for-insurance-agents)
- Edelman–LinkedIn 2025 B2B Thought Leadership Impact Report (n=1,934 US management-level execs, Mar–Apr 2025): 64% of hidden decision-makers and 65% of target decision-makers trust thought leadership more than marketing materials for assessing capabilities; 79% of hidden decision-makers more likely to advocate for firms with strong thought leadership in RFPs; 53% say good thought leadership reduces the importance of brand recognition; preferences: 65% human/informal tone, 57% quick takeaways over academic content, 60% value unique format — [Edelman-LinkedIn 2025 PDF](https://www.edelman.com/sites/g/files/aatuss191/files/2025-07/2025%20Edelman-LinkedIn%20B2B%20Thought%20Leadership%20Impact%20Report_FINAL.pdf)
- Short-form video delivers the highest ROI among video formats for 41% of B2B marketers (LinkedIn B2B Marketing Benchmark 2025, cited by Sprout) — [Sprout Social](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/social-media-statistics/)
- Brazil context: 82% of Brazilian consumers have been targeted by digital fraud; fake ads (45%) and fake profiles (28%) mostly on Facebook/Instagram; 70% abandoned online purchases over fraud fears (Branddi, n=500, Sep 2025) — [Forbes Brasil](https://forbes.com.br/forbes-tech/2025/09/82-dos-consumidores-ja-foram-alvo-de-fraude-digital-e-o-maior-perigo-esta-nas-redes-sociais/)
- Compliance: use pre-approved content libraries, route flagged content for approval, disclose influencer partnerships (FCA/FINRA/IIROC examples) — [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/insurance-social-media/)
- Testimonials require written client permission; claim stories should be anonymised — [Agger](https://www.agger.com.br/blog/reels-para-corretoras-de-seguros/); [Sendible](https://www.sendible.com/insights/social-media-for-insurance-agents)

### Inferences
- Content explaining sinistro processes, why premiums rose (e.g., auto repair costs, climate events) and what policies exclude is directly supported by J.D. Power's finding that understanding neutralises price-driven trust loss.
- In Brazil, verification trust signals matter more than usual: verified profile, SUSEP registration number, real team faces, physical address, consistent handle and warnings about fraud can differentiate a legitimate corretora.
- For corporate clients on LinkedIn, the broker's personal profile posting concise, informal, practical risk insights (e.g., D&O, cyber, frota, responsabilidade civil) is supported by Edelman data on tone and format.
- DM response speed is itself a public trust signal and a conversion step (social → WhatsApp/phone).

### Gaps
- No quantified study isolating the engagement/conversion lift of human faces vs graphics for insurance accounts.
- No Brazil-specific survey found on how consumers use social media to choose a corretor (CNseg/SUSEP data not located).

## 4. Recommended posting frequency and mix for a small brokerage

### Takeaway
Benchmarks show brands posting ~4 times/week on Instagram (FinServ 4.29/week) and practitioner guidance for agents is 3–5 posts/week on Instagram/Facebook, including 2–3 Reels/week; on LinkedIn, ~2–4 posts/week mixing documents, multi-image and short video. Mix should be ~80% value (education/trust) and ~20% promotional, with consistency prioritised over volume.

### Cited Findings
- Financial Services brands post ~4.3 times/week on Instagram and Facebook — [Rival IQ PDF](https://get.rivaliq.com/hubfs/eBooks/2025-Social-Media-Industry-Benchmark-Report.pdf)
- Cross-industry Instagram brands: ~20 posts/month (8 Reels, 7 images, 5 carousels) — [Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/instagram)
- Agents: 3–5 posts/week on Facebook and Instagram; 80/20 educational/promotional — [Sendible](https://www.sendible.com/insights/social-media-for-insurance-agents)
- Brazilian corretoras: 2–3 Reels/week, consistency over volume, monthly batch recording — [Agger](https://www.agger.com.br/blog/reels-para-corretoras-de-seguros/)
- LinkedIn brand pages: ~7 images, 4 videos, 2 documents, 1 text per month in 2025 — [Socialinsider LinkedIn](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/linkedin)
- Measure DMs, calls, conversations and CTR rather than vanity metrics — [Sendible](https://www.sendible.com/insights/social-media-for-insurance-agents)

### Inferences
- Suggested starting mix for a small brokerage (Instagram, ~4 posts/week): 2 Reels (myth-busting, sinistro explainer, broker on camera, seasonal risk), 1–2 carousels (checklists/coverage comparisons/"o que seu seguro não cobre"), 1 trust post (testimonial, team/backstage, claim resolved) — plus daily Stories for service/Q&A. LinkedIn: 2 posts/week from the broker's profile (document carousel + short insight/video) on corporate risks.
- Seasonal hooks (rainy season/enchentes, holidays/travel, Black Friday theft, year-end car trips) provide a recurring calendar of risk tips.
- CTA should be low-friction and single: "save/send to someone", "comment X", or "chame no WhatsApp/DM" — aligned with sends-per-reach and the call/DM conversion advantage.

### Gaps
- No controlled evidence on the optimal frequency specifically for small insurance accounts; figures are averages and practitioner recommendations.
